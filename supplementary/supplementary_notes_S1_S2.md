# Supplementary Notes

Brief Report: *The naked mole-rat cGAS "longevity" residues are an ancestral, weakly constrained hystricognath state shared with guinea pigs.*

---

## Supplementary Note S1. Protein language model (ESM-2) scoring of the four positions

**Purpose and limits.** A protein language model trained on millions of natural sequences assigns every amino acid at every position a likelihood given the rest of the protein. Substitutions the model strongly disfavours are enriched for destabilising or function-disrupting changes. The model has no notion of longevity and no access to phenotype; a high or low score is therefore evidence about sequence plausibility only, never about a longevity function. We report the analysis because a strong signal either way would have been worth knowing, and because the negative result should be on record.

**Method.** ESM-2 (650 M parameters, `esm2_t33_650M_UR50D`; Lin et al. 2023) was run on the human cGAS sequence (UniProt Q8N884, 522 aa) and on 262 mammalian cGAS sequences. For each of the four positions we computed the single-substitution log-likelihood ratio (LLR) of the naked mole-rat residue against the human wild-type residue in the human background, with the position masked. We also scored the reverse substitutions in the naked mole-rat background, and computed a per-species pseudo-log-likelihood normalised by length (PLL/L), both for the whole protein and restricted to the region aligning to human 161–522, for the 204 species with a longevity quotient (LQ) in AnAge Build 15.

**Results.** In the human background the LLRs are D431S +0.80, K479E −2.12, L495Y +0.03 and K498T −1.63. None falls in the range typical of clearly damaging substitutions (conventionally LLR below about −5 for this model), and two are positive. Placed against the 19 alternative residues at each position, the naked mole-rat residue ranks 2nd of 20 at 431, 8th at 479, 15th at 495 and 9th at 498. At 431 and 495 the naked mole-rat residue is among the residues the model finds unremarkable; at 479 and 498 the model still prefers the human residue, and it continues to do so when scored in the naked mole-rat background — that is, the naked mole-rat state runs mildly against the model's general preference at these two positions. If any of the four is to be tested first on sequence grounds alone, 479 and 498 are the candidates.

**Species-level scores do not track longevity.** The length-normalised pseudo-likelihood of whole cGAS does not correlate with LQ across 204 species (Spearman ρ = 0.06), nor with maximum lifespan (ρ = −0.04); restricted to human positions 161–522 the correlations are ρ = 0.11 and 0.12. Medians by lifespan tertile are indistinguishable (long-lived −1.872, intermediate −1.854, short-lived −1.853). Within orders the sign is inconsistent (Artiodactyla ρ = +0.47, Primates ρ = −0.34), which is what one expects from a quantity driven mainly by sequence composition and alignment quality. The highest-scoring species are the monotremes and several *Myotis* bats, i.e. the model is reporting sequence typicality, not biology.

**Conclusion.** ESM-2 neither supports nor contradicts a special role for the four residues. The analysis is reported here and is not used to support any claim in the main text.

## Supplementary Note S2. codeml analysis of position 495 across rodents, and why the branch-site test is circular here

**Motivation.** Position 495 is the one site of the four whose ancestral state is not stable across reconstruction methods (Results, section 4) and which changes repeatedly within Hystricognathi. We therefore asked, separately from the mammal-wide FEL analysis, whether the Y495 gains carry a detectable signal of positive selection within rodents.

**Data and models.** We used the final cGAS exon (naked mole-rat codons 438–554 = human 406–522, 117 codons), the one region recoverable as a single tblastn high-scoring pair from unannotated assemblies, for 68 rodents (50 RefSeq coding sequences; 17 hystricognaths and the gundi from genomic fragments retrieved at the tblastn hit coordinates). Every sequence was checked by translating the codons and confirming that the residue at naked mole-rat position 527 matched the call in the site table. The tree was the Upham et al. (2019) topology pruned to these 68 species and unrooted. Because estimating 133 branch lengths jointly made each model run for hours, branch lengths were estimated once under M0 and then held fixed for the site and branch-site models — standard practice, but it makes the likelihood-ratio tests mildly anti-conservative. Foreground branches for the branch-site test were specified in advance as the five branches on which Y495 is reconstructed to arise (African mole-rat stem, *Cavia* stem, the chinchilla terminal branch, the octodontoid stem, the Cuban hutia) plus the *Sciurus* stem, six in total.

**Results.** M0 gives ω = 0.42 for the exon as a whole. M8 versus M7 is highly significant (2ΔlnL = 47.8, p = 4 × 10⁻¹¹), as is M8 versus M8a (2ΔlnL = 26.5, p = 1 × 10⁻⁷): about 16% of codons in this exon sit in a class with ω ≈ 1.6. Position 495 is in that class, with Bayes empirical Bayes posterior 0.995 and posterior mean ω = 1.50. It is not, however, distinctive: 21 positions in the same 117 codons have posterior > 0.6 and 13 exceed 0.95, among them human 427 (a ubiquitination site), 451 and 454 (herpesvirus deamidation sites), 505 and 506 (a methylation site). The branch-site test is marginal (A vs. A-null 2ΔlnL = 3.06, p = 0.040), with foreground ω estimated at 6.9 over 2.5% of sites; 495 is the highest-posterior foreground site but only at 0.87.

**Interpretation.** Two things argue against reading this as selection for a longevity function. First, the C-terminal tail of cGAS is a rapidly evolving region across rodents generally, consistent with cGAS's role in antiviral conflict — several of the co-identified positions are known virus-targeted or post-translationally modified residues. Second, and decisively, the branch-site foreground was *defined by the substitution being tested*: we selected the branches on which 495 changed and then asked whether 495 shows elevated ω on those branches. With a fixed-branch-length approximation and p = 0.040, this is not evidence. Power is also low: 117 codons, six short foreground branches, a single site of interest. We therefore report the result and do not use it. The repeated appearance of Y495 is better read as recurrent substitution at a fast-evolving site than as repeated selection of the same residue for longevity.
